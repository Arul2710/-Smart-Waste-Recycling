Understanding Scrap Rates in India: A Complete Guide

RMRohit Mehra Aug 28, 2026 7 min read

The quiet economics of your bin

Every month, the average Indian household discards between 8 and 12 kilograms of material that a scrap dealer would happily buy. The catch has never been the material — it is the friction: heavy bags, opaque rates and guessing games at the weight.

EcoScrap removes that friction by publishing rates, calibrating scales and paying on the spot. This article walks through how those published numbers are actually decided, and how you can use that knowledge to earn more from every pickup.

Why metal prices swing every day

Copper, aluminium and steel follow international futures markets plus port-side demand in India. A spike on the London Metal Exchange usually shows up in local dealer boards within 24 hours. Paper, by contrast, moves on domestic pulp demand and festive season packaging cycles.

What actually changes your payout

  • Separation. Copper sorted away from steel prices as copper; mixed metal prices as the lowest common denominator.
  • Moisture. Wet paper is heavy paper — payouts are based on material, not water weight.
  • Contamination. Food residue, plastic-coated boards and glued laminates knock paper into lower grades.
  • Verified weight. A calibrated scale versus a hand guess is often a 10–15% difference.

Reading a rate card like a pro

The published rate is a starting point. For clean, dry, well-separated loads you are entitled to the top of the band. Ask for a category-specific estimate before the collector arrives — it makes the weighing conversation honest and fast.

The takeaway

Scrap value is real money. A modest household that bundles responsibly can add a few thousand rupees a year from material it would otherwise lose — while the environment keeps the material in circulation.
